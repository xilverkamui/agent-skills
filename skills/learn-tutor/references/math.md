# Math reference (SMP grades 8-9)

Read this for any math question. The core rules in `SKILL.md` still apply.

## 1. Curriculum variants

**National (default).** Kurikulum Merdeka. Four strands: number, algebra, geometry and measurement, data and probability. Topic order differs by textbook, so follow the student's textbook or teacher.

Typical SMP 8-9 topics (indicative, not exhaustive):

- **Number:** integers and operations, fractions and decimals, ratio and proportion, percentages, exponents and roots, number patterns, sequences.
- **Algebra:** algebraic expressions, linear equations and inequalities in one variable, relations and functions, linear functions and straight-line equations, systems of linear equations in two variables.
- **Geometry and measurement:** angles, triangles and quadrilaterals, Pythagorean theorem, circles, congruence and similarity, transformations, surface area and volume of prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, spheres.
- **Data and probability:** data presentation, mean, median, mode, range, simple probability.

**Cambridge Lower Secondary (Stages 7-9).** Strands are number, algebra, geometry and measure, statistics and probability, plus problem solving. Check the student's actual syllabus when it matters.

What changes in practice:

- English terms and instructions ("work out", "show that", "write down", "calculate", "explain").
- **Show working.** Method matters, not just the final answer.
- Decimal point, not decimal comma.
- Give the final answer with units where relevant, and keep the student's rounding instruction ("to 2 decimal places", "to 3 significant figures").

**Notation, national context.** Decimal comma (3,5), dot as thousands separator (Rp1.000.000). Division may be written with ":" or the division sign, so accept both. Equations have "ruas kiri" and "ruas kanan". When the curriculum is unknown, mirror the notation the student used.

## 2. Hint ladder

Start with the smallest useful hint. Move up only when the student is still stuck. Do not keep withholding once they clearly need the solution or ask for it.

1. **Conceptual:** point at the idea. "Coba pikirkan, operasi apa yang merupakan kebalikan dari +5?"
2. **Strategic:** point at the method. "Kita ingin membuat x berdiri sendiri. Apa yang perlu dilakukan terhadap +5?"
3. **First step:** show the first meaningful step. "Kita kurangi 5 pada kedua ruas, sehingga menjadi 2x = 10."
4. **Guided completion:** help them finish the remaining steps, asking them to do the important ones.
5. **Full solution** with reasoning, then offer a similar problem.

## 3. Solution format

Use this structure for multi-step or word problems. Skip it for simple questions.

- **Diketahui:** the relevant information.
- **Ditanyakan:** what to find.
- **Strategi:** the concept or method, in one line.
- **Penyelesaian:** step by step calculation, each step with its reason.
- **Jawaban:** final result with units.
- **Cek:** a quick verification (substitution, estimation, inverse operation), when useful.

Cambridge variant: same logic in English, written as clear working lines, with the final answer labelled and units included.

## 4. Checking a student's answer

1. State whether it is correct.
2. Show a brief verification when useful (substitute back, estimate, inverse operation).
3. Name what was right, locate the exact error, explain why it is wrong.
4. Name the concept involved and invite a retry.

Partial credit helps: "Ide dasarnya sudah tepat. Yang perlu diperbaiki adalah penggunaan satuannya."

## 5. Common misconceptions to watch for

- Sign errors with negative numbers; subtracting a negative.
- Adding fractions by adding numerators and denominators.
- Doing an operation on only one side of an equation.
- (a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2.
- Percent change taken from the wrong base.
- Mixing length and area units (cm versus cm^2).
- Using the Pythagorean theorem on a triangle that is not right-angled, or picking the wrong side as the hypotenuse.
- Confusing area with perimeter.
- Mean versus median versus mode.
- Probability as favorable over unfavorable instead of favorable over total.
- Slope sign and slope versus intercept.
- Order of operations; distributing a negative sign.

If a misconception repeats, go back to the prerequisite briefly, with concrete numbers or a picture, then return to the problem.

## 6. Practice

- One meaningful problem at a time. Wait for the answer, evaluate it, explain the important reasoning, then adjust the next problem.
- Vary numbers, context, representation, and reasoning. Avoid long lists of near-identical drills unless asked.
- The goal is understanding, not repetitive calculation.
- Raise difficulty only after consistent success, not after one right answer.

## 7. Contexts and visuals

- Contexts: rupiah and shopping, discounts, travel distance and speed, recipes and proportions, school grades, room dimensions, sports statistics.
- Visuals: tables, number lines, coordinate sketches, simple text diagrams. If a diagram cannot render reliably, describe it clearly or use a text sketch.

## 8. Accuracy checklist before sending

- Recompute every number.
- Re-check each algebraic transformation.
- Check units and notation.
- Substitute the answer back into the original problem.
- Confirm the final answer actually answers what was asked.
